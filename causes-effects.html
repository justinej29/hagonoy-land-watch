Understanding the drivers

Causes and Effects

Land subsidence in Hagonoy is not caused by a single factor. It is the result of interconnected human and natural forces that amplify one another and have been building for decades.

Cause 1

Groundwater Extraction

The most significant driver of land subsidence in Hagonoy is the large-scale extraction of groundwater from underground aquifers. As water is pumped out for household use, fish pond operations, and agriculture, the water pressure that normally holds soil particles apart is reduced, causing the sediment layers to compact and the ground to sink.

Cause

Over-Extraction of Groundwater

Thousands of deep wells serve households, fish ponds, and farms across Hagonoy. Demand far exceeds the natural recharge rate of local aquifers.

Effect

Aquifer Depletion and Soil Compaction

As underground water is removed, pore pressure drops. Soft clay and silt layers compress irreversibly, reducing soil volume and lowering the surface above.

Impact

Ground Sinking, Flooding, Salt Intrusion

Land drops below sea level, flooding becomes more frequent and severe, and seawater invades freshwater wells and fish ponds, destroying livelihoods.

Cause 2

Worsening Floods

Subsidence and flooding form a destructive feedback loop. As the land sinks, flooding becomes more frequent and more severe. Floodwaters saturate the already soft soils, accelerating further compaction and making the area even more flood-prone, which creates a cycle that is difficult to break without addressing the root cause.

Cause

Land Surface Below Flood Level

Subsidence lowers the elevation of residential areas and farmland. Roads, yards, and fields that were once above flood lines are now inundated during moderate rainfall events.

Effect

Drainage Systems Fail

As ground elevation drops, gravity-fed drainage systems lose their gradient and can no longer effectively drain water away. Pump-dependent drainage becomes the only option, which is expensive and unreliable during power outages.

Impact

Prolonged Inundation and Health Risks

Floodwaters remain longer, contaminating drinking water, damaging homes and crops, spreading waterborne diseases like leptospirosis and typhoid, and forcing families to evacuate.

Cause 3

Infrastructure Damage

The uneven and progressive sinking of the ground exerts enormous stress on structures built above it. Buildings, roads, bridges, and water pipes all suffer when the ground beneath them moves at different rates, a phenomenon called differential subsidence.

Cause

Differential Ground Settlement

Different areas sink at different rates depending on soil composition, depth to bedrock, and local pumping intensity. This uneven movement creates stress fractures in rigid structures.

Effect

Cracked Walls, Broken Pipes, Damaged Roads

Structural cracks appear in buildings and bridges. Underground pipes fracture, causing water loss and sewage leaks. Road surfaces heave, buckle, or develop sinkholes.

Impact

High Repair Costs and Safety Hazards

Communities and local government must bear the cost of constant repairs. In some cases, structures become unsafe for occupancy, forcing displacement and adding to the financial burden of already vulnerable families.

Additional drivers

Other Contributing Factors

Beyond groundwater extraction, several other factors accelerate land subsidence in Hagonoy and the broader Manila Bay coastal zone.

Source: Hagonoy Battle against Flooding

Construction Load

The weight of new buildings, roads, and infrastructure on soft deltaic soils adds direct mechanical pressure, compressing the saturated sediment layers below and accelerating subsidence in developed areas.

Source: Pampanga River Delta

Natural Consolidation

River deltas are naturally dynamic systems. Young sediments deposited by rivers undergo long-term consolidation as water is squeezed out over time, a natural process that was occurring even before human settlement.

Source: Rappler

Sea-Level Rise

Climate change is causing global sea levels to rise at an accelerating rate. When combined with land subsidence, coastal areas experience a compound effect known as relative sea-level rise, which is far more severe than either factor alone.

Source: Instagram

Loss of Wetlands and Mangroves

Coastal wetlands and mangrove forests act as natural buffers, stabilizing soils and absorbing wave energy. Their conversion to fishponds and residential areas removes this protection and accelerates erosion and subsidence at the shoreline.
