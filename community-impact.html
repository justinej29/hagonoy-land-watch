Real lives, real effects

Community Impact

Land subsidence does not just affect the ground. It touches homes, work, health, and the future of every family in Hagonoy.

Where people live

Homes and Residential Areas

For most families in Hagonoy, their home is their most important asset. When the ground beneath it shifts, the costs can be both financial and emotional, and can be devastating.

Source: newsinfo.inquirer.net

Structural Damage

Cracks appear in walls and floors. Doors and windows jam. Foundations tilt. In severe cases a house may become unsafe to live in and require costly repair or demolition.

Source: dutchwatersector.com

Repeated Flooding

Homes near rivers flood more often as the ground sinks. Families spend days cleaning up and replacing damaged belongings, with no lasting solution in sight.

Source: facebook.com

Loss of Property Value

A home in a frequently flooded area is worth less. Selling becomes difficult. Some families find themselves trapped in a place that is slowly becoming unlivable.

"Every time there's a typhoon, the water now comes up to the door. Before, it never reached the house. We had to put our furniture on top of tables."

Barangay-Level Examples

Barangay San Sebastian is one of the clearest local examples of how subsidence and tidal flooding combine. Its public cemetery has been flooded for almost a decade, even on days without rain or typhoons, and the municipality has had to place sandbags and flood barriers along the nearby river. Municipal officials also report that the town as a whole is sinking by about 10 cm a year, which keeps pushing the tide line further into low-lying barangays like this one.

Residents of San Sebastian, including its youth leaders, have said that flooding here has been treated as normal for too long. This example shows how the problem reaches not only homes and roads but also community spaces such as cemeteries and schools.

Sources: Manila Bulletin (Oct 2025), Inquirer Plus,

Work and income

Livelihoods: Fishponds and Farming

Hagonoy has a long tradition of fishing, aquaculture, and rice farming. Land subsidence puts all of these livelihoods at serious risk.

Source: facebook.com

Fishpond Flooding and Damage

As the ground sinks, fishpond dikes are more likely to be overtopped or breached during floods. Fish escape and ponds need expensive repair each time.

Source: Sciencedirect.com

Saltwater Intrusion Into Ponds

When seawater flows into freshwater fishponds, freshwater species die. Farmers must change fish species or abandon the pond entirely, which represents a major financial loss.

Source: facebook.com

Rice Farm Waterlogging

Paddy fields that sit lower than before hold water too long after rains. This damages crops, shortens the growing season, and reduces harvests and family income.

Source: facebook.com

Higher Costs for Farmers

Dike repairs, pump maintenance, and crop losses add up. Farming income drops while costs rise. Some families are forced to seek work outside Hagonoy.

"We used to harvest twice a year from our fishpond. Now, after bad typhoons, we spend months just fixing the dikes. We barely break even."

Getting around

Roads and Infrastructure

Roads and bridges connect Hagonoy's barangays to each other and to the rest of Bulacan. When the ground sinks, these lifelines weaken.

Road Surface Damage

Roads in low-lying areas crack and buckle as the ground shifts beneath them. Patches wear out faster than on stable ground. During floods, submerged roads are harder to navigate safely. Emergency vehicles such as ambulances and fire trucks may be delayed, putting lives at risk.

Water and Drainage Systems

Underground water pipes and drainage canals designed for a specific slope no longer drain correctly once the ground settles unevenly. This leads to sewer backups, standing water, and risks of waterborne disease from contaminated water sources.

Education and well-being

Schools, Health, and Safety

The effects of land subsidence reach into schools and health centres, the places communities depend on most.

Source: share.google.com

Schools as Evacuation Centres

When flooding gets severe, school buildings become evacuation shelters. This interrupts classes for days or weeks, causing students to miss crucial learning time.

Source: www1.racgp.org.au

Waterborne Illness

Floodwater often carries sewage and disease-causing bacteria. Children and elderly residents are most at risk from waterborne illnesses such as leptospirosis and diarrhoea.

Source: theknowfresno.org

Stress and Mental Health

Constant threat of flooding causes significant stress. Families that repeatedly lose belongings experience lasting emotional and financial strain that is hard to recover from.

Source: philstar.com

Disrupted Supply Chains

Flooded roads delay food, medicine, and other supplies. Prices rise in isolated barangays during and after flood events, hitting the poorest families hardest.

Barangay-Level Example: Palapat

Palapat, in Hagonoy East District, was among the barangays whose schools stayed flood-free in earlier reports, but a local councilor has noted that even upland areas like Palapat have recently reported flooding. Federico C. Suntay Elementary School serves this community, and its pupils are affected whenever the municipality suspends classes. In late August and early September 2026, Hagonoy suspended face-to-face classes for all levels, public and private, because of habagat flooding.

The loss of learning time is felt across the town. During the second week of School Year 2026-2027, tides of 4.9 to 5.2 feet forced many Hagonoy schools to switch to modular and online learning, and at least one school reported families moving their children elsewhere to escape the flooding.

Sources: The Manila Times, Philstar,

There Are Things We Can Do

These problems are serious but not hopeless. Individual actions, community programmes, and government measures can all help reduce the impact.
